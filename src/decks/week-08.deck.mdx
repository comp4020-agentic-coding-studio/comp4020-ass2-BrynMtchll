# The puzzle

---

## Corvids and parrots

Doing things comparable to great apes.

With brains a fraction of the size.

A deep problem for any information-processing view.

---

## Olkowicz et al., 2016

Isotropic fractionator. 28 avian species. Count the neurons.

---

## The answer

Parrot and songbird brains carry **roughly twice** the neurons of primate brains
of equal mass.

And a higher proportion sits in the forebrain.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

They were never small in the currency that matters.

---

## The complementary argument

Herculano-Houzel — a co-author on that paper — on us:

the human brain is an **ordinary primate brain scaled up**. No special
encephalisation.

What is unusual is the cortical neuron count, and the metabolic budget to run it.

---

## And in insects

Chittka & Niven, 2009.

Differentiated behaviour, social structure and something like categorisation
need far fewer neurons than anyone assumed.

# The best experiment on this course

---

## *Cataglyphis fortis*

Path integration.

Direction from polarised skylight. Distance from **counting its own strides**.

---

## Wittlinger, Wehner & Wolf, 2006

After the outbound run, glue pig-bristle **stilts** to the legs.

Or trim the legs to **stumps**.

---

## Result

| Manipulation | Homeward run |
| --- | --- |
| Stilts, after outbound | overshoots the nest |
| Stumps, after outbound | undershoots |
| Either, **before** outbound | correct |

Same stride counted both ways. It's a pedometer.

---

## What is stored

A **vector**. Not a route.

So it always knows where home is, and never knows how it got where it is.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

An ant that is lost behaves exactly like an ant that is not.
