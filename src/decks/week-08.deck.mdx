# Part 1 — the puzzle

---

## Darwin already had it, 1871

{/* _class: quote */}

"The brain of an ant is one of the most marvellous atoms of matter in the
world, perhaps more so than the brain of man."

---

## And the intuition it cuts against

<div class="cols">
<div>

| System | Mass | Neurons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blue whale | up to 9 kg | >200 billion |
| Human | 1250–1450 g | ~85 billion |
| Honeybee | ~1 mm³ | **fewer than 10⁶** |

</div>
<div>

Corvids and parrots perform comparably to great apes on some tasks.

Bees learn abstract rules.

A million-fold shortfall in hardware, and not a million-fold shortfall in
behaviour.

</div>
</div>

Counts from Chittka & Niven, *Current Biology* 19(21), 2009.

---

## An old and bad answer

**1850** — Dujardin proposes the *mushroom bodies* are the "organs of
intelligence" in arthropods, partly because they are enlarged in social
insects.

**1929** — Pandazis notes the largest mushroom bodies belong to **horseshoe
crabs**.

```notes
Let the second date land. The structure-equals-faculty move fails immediately.
```

---

## Pandazis's own resolution

Size doesn't matter; **wiring** does.

Insect mushroom bodies contain cells with extensive dendritic ramifications and
connections to all major brain centres — as a centre of higher-order
integration requires. *Limulus* mushroom bodies do not.

That is the move this whole lecture makes, repeatedly.

# Part 2 — open the bee

---

## Where a bee's neurons actually go

![Two panels. Left, a single stacked bar of a honeybee's roughly one million neurons, dominated by the optic lobes. Right, the same components on a logarithmic axis: optic lobes 648,000, mushroom bodies 340,000, protocerebrum 19,000, antennal lobes 4,500, motor neurons 2,500.](./figures/bee-neuron-budget.svg)

Counts from Chittka & Niven 2009, Fig. 1.

---

## Do the arithmetic

```
optic lobes      3 × 216,000  =  648,000
mushroom bodies  2 × 170,000  =  340,000
protocerebrum                 =   19,000
antennal lobes                =    4,500
motor neurons                 =    2,500
                                ---------
                                  1,014,000
```

**Sanity check:** Chittka & Niven give "fewer than a million neurons" for a
~1 mm³ brain. The parts add to about that, so we have the whole budget and not
a subset of it.

---

## Read the first line again

```
648,000 / 1,014,000  =  0.64
```

**64% of the budget is optic lobes.**

Lamina, medulla and lobula: ~216,000 cells *each*.

Two-thirds of the brain is spent before any integration happens at all.

---

## What is left to think with

| Region | Neurons | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mushroom bodies | ~170,000 × 2 | learning and memory |
| Protocerebrum remainder | ~19,000 | integration, projections to pre-motor |
| Antennal lobes | ~4,500 in ~160 glomeruli | olfaction |
| Deutocerebrum | ~2,500 | motor output |

Nineteen thousand neurons, for everything the mushroom bodies don't do.

---

## And it runs 59 distinct behaviours

Chittka and Niven enumerate them. A sample:

*aggressive flight · alarm fanning · antennation · attend dance · beg food ·
brief piping · brood incubation · build comb · buzz run · cap brood · colony
fission · corpse removal · dorsoventral abdominal vibration · expulsion of
drones · guarding · lateral shake · robbing other hives · scouting for food ·
sickle dance · sterzeln · tremble dance · waggle dance · worker policing*

```notes
Read six of these out loud. The list is the argument; paraphrase kills it.
```

# Part 3 — so what does a big brain buy?

---

## Mostly: more of the same

<div class="cols">
<div>

**Visual sampling**

| Eye | Sampling units |
| --- | --- |
| Fruit fly eye | 700 ommatidia |
| Large dragonfly | 30,000 |
| Human fovea alone | 60,000 cones |
| Human retina | ~400,000 "pixels" |

</div>
<div>

**Olfactory sampling**

| Species | Glomeruli |
| --- | --- |
| Fruit fly | 43 |
| Honeybee | ~160 |
| Human | ~350 |
| Mouse | ~1000 |

</div>
</div>

Finer resolution, higher sensitivity, more precision. Not new operations.

---

## The same pattern centrally

Primary visual cortex area:

**mouse 4.5 mm²  →  macaque 1200 mm²  →  human 3000 mm²**

A ~670-fold increase, largely engaged in low-level feature extraction, without
necessarily increasing the *diversity* of ways information is processed.

---

## Convergence worth noticing

Insects and vertebrates arrived at the same architecture independently:
**retinotopic maps** built from local circuits — "cartridges" or columns —
repeated hundreds or thousands of times.

Same solution, and no common ancestor that had it.

# Part 4 — and the motor system isn't simpler

---

## Skeletal muscle counts

| Animal | Muscles |
| --- | --- |
| Weasel | 322 |
| Primates | 316 |
| **Migratory locust** | **296** |
| Rat, hare | ~215 |
| Opossum | 159 |

An insect sits inside the mammalian range. Chittka & Niven 2009, Fig. 3.

---

## With strikingly few neurons controlling them

Desert locust flight:

- **72 motor neurons** innervating **34 muscles**
- the flight central pattern generator is **3 neurons**

Vertebrate limb muscles are typically innervated by tens of motor neurons;
highly innervated insect muscles have fewer than fifteen.

---

## Why that matters for the account

Novel behaviour does not require novel hardware.

It can come from few neurons **recruiting existing motor circuits in a
different order** — which is cheap, and which is why repertoire size does not
track brain size.

# Part 6 — but is it cognition?

---

## Delayed matching-to-sample, in a bee

Train: see pattern **A**, then choose between **A** and **B**. A is rewarded.

Then: bees generalise to *"choose the same one"* with entirely different
stimuli — pattern **C**, then C and D — having never seen them.

They transfer the sameness rule **across modalities**, from olfactory
entrainment to visual test. And they learn the inverse: *choose the odd one*.

---

## Two objections you must be able to state

<div class="cols">
<div>

**Horridge**

Bees may extract only low-level cues, edge orientation, contour length,
spectral content — and never represent "sameness" at all.

</div>
<div>

**Dennett's *Sphex***

The digger wasp, its provisioning interrupted, repeats the whole routine
indefinitely. Complex behaviour, no comprehension.

</div>
</div>

Grade the sameness-concept claim **[C]** and say what would move it.

---

## Srinivasan's inversion

The interesting possibility isn't that bees manage rules *despite* small brains.

It's that rule learning, concept formation and categorisation may be adaptive
**because** of miniaturisation, combining objects under one category economises
on memory, rather than storing each instance.

Constraint as the cause of abstraction. Keep that for week 12.

# Part 7 — count the right thing

---

## Olkowicz et al., 2016

28 avian species, isotropic fractionator.

- Parrot and songbird brains hold **roughly twice** the neurons of primate brains of equal mass
- A **higher proportion** sits in the pallial telencephalon
- So large parrots and corvids match or exceed monkeys with far larger brains on forebrain neuron count

*PNAS* 113(26), 7255–7260.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

They were never small in the currency that matters.

---

## Which is what this part is for

Our intuitions about which minds are capable were tracking the wrong quantity
for decades.

We found that out by measuring a lineage **from outside it**. Nobody discovered
it by introspecting about brains.

---

## And the same argument, pointed at us

Herculano-Houzel — a co-author on that paper — on the human brain:

an **ordinary primate brain scaled up**, with no special encephalisation.

What is unusual is the absolute cortical neuron count, and the metabolic budget
required to run it.

```notes
Also kill the 10:1 glia myth here if anyone raises it. It is about 1:1.
```

# Part 8 — the ant

---

## Two quantities, two mechanisms

*Cataglyphis fortis*, Saharan salt-pan forager. No pheromone trail — too hot,
too volatile.

| Quantity | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Direction | celestial compass, polarised skylight |
| Distance | **a stride integrator** |

The second one is the claim that needed proving.

---

## The design is the argument

![A flow diagram: ants trained to a feeder complete an outbound run, then have their legs altered. Stilts produce overshoot and stumps produce undershoot, because the same stride count is applied to a different stride length. Ants altered before the outbound run navigate correctly. The conclusion is that distance is measured by counting strides.](./figures/stilts-and-stumps.svg)

Wittlinger, Wehner & Wolf, *Science* 312, 1965–1967 (2006).

---

## Work the stilts

Say an ant walks **10 m** out with a natural stride of **10 mm**. Its
integrator therefore holds:

```
strides counted  =  10.0 m / 0.010 m  =  1,000
```

Glue on stilts so the stride becomes **13 mm**, then let it run home. It runs
until the count is repaid:

```
distance covered  =  1,000 × 0.013 m  =  13.0 m
overshoot         =  13.0 − 10.0      =   3.0 m
```

---

## And the stumps

Trim the legs so the stride becomes **7 mm**:

```
distance covered  =  1,000 × 0.007 m  =   7.0 m
undershoot        =  10.0 − 7.0       =   3.0 m
```

**Sanity check:** the error is proportional to the stride change, and to the
outbound distance. A longer foraging trip makes the same manipulation worse in
absolute terms — which is what the experiments found.

---

## The moral

The ant is not measuring distance. It is **counting a proxy** and multiplying
by an assumed constant.

Change the constant after the count and the arithmetic is still perfect. The
answer is simply wrong, and nothing inside the calculation can notice.

---

## Why the control is the clever part

Manipulating **before** the outbound run leaves navigation intact.

The ant counts the same stride length in both directions, so the error cancels.

That single condition rules out optic flow, energy expenditure and elapsed time
in one move.

```notes
Ask them to design the control before showing it. Most propose a sham operation,
which does not dissociate the mechanism.
```

---

## Robust in ways that are informative

The distance estimate survives:

- carrying heavy prey
- walking backwards
- losing one or two legs

It's a **stride integrator** that compensates for stride length and walking
speed, not a naive step counter. Follow-up: *J Exp Biol* 210(2), 198–207 (2007).

---

## And bees do it completely differently

Walking insects integrate strides. **Flying insects use optic flow.**

Same computational problem, two unrelated solutions, chosen by what the body
makes available.

# What is stored is a vector

---

## Not a route

<div class="cols">
<div>

**A route** would let you retrace, cross-check, and recognise where you had
been.

**A vector** is a bearing and a distance, continuously updated.

</div>
<div>

So the ant always knows where home is —

and never knows how it got where it is.

</div>
</div>

---

## Which has a consequence you can compute

![Two panels. Left, a simulated wandering outbound path from the nest, with the true direction home and the slightly wrong homeward vector the ant actually runs, missing by a couple of body lengths. Right, mean miss distance against outbound path length, crossing the width of the nest entrance at around fifty steps.](./figures/path-integration-error.svg)

Simulation: per-step heading error SD 6°, step-length error SD 4%, seeded.

---

## The result is not what you would guess

The integrator is **good**. Errors partly cancel on a wandering path, so the
miss grows roughly as √n and stays within a few body lengths.

It is still not good enough — **because the target is a hole.**

Past ~50 steps the expected error exceeds the nest entrance.

---

## So the spiral is not a failure

Arriving in the right place stops being the same as finding it.

The systematic search at the end of the homeward run is the **only available
remedy** for an accumulator with no external reference.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

An ant that is lost behaves exactly like an ant that is not.

---

## Take that sentence to week 12

No independent reference means no error signal.

No error signal means the failure is **invisible from inside**, and no amount of
better engineering within that design removes it.

You can see the ant's bound because you are outside it.

# Part 9 — two things with no obvious function

---

## Bumblebees roll balls

Galpayage Dona et al., *Animal Behaviour* 194, 239–251 (2022).

- 45 tagged bees; individuals rolled between **1 and 117** times
- they **detoured** into a ball chamber past a clear path to food
- a second experiment (42 bees) showed colour-chamber preference for balls
- younger bees rolled more; males rolled for longer

No proboscis extension, no biting — so not food-directed. Frequency rose then
fell, matching play rather than exploration.

---

## Jumping spiders may dream

Rößler et al., *PNAS* 119(33), e2204754119 (2022).

The method is the finding: salticids cannot move their lenses, but they *can*
move their retinal tubes, and newly emerged spiderlings are unpigmented
enough to watch it happen.

34 spiders showed regular bouts of retinal movement with limb and spinneret
twitching, lengthening through the night.

---

## Grade both honestly

**[C]** and **[C]**.

Note the asymmetry even here. We can state precisely what evidence would settle
the question for a bee.

For the same question about ourselves, we cannot say what would count.

Neither tells us what we want it to. "Play" and "REM" are both defined by
criteria developed on mammals, and the question of whether those criteria
travel is exactly the question.

Seminar: what evidence would move either?

# What this week established

---

## Four things

1. **A small brain is not a scaled-down large one** — it is differently
   apportioned, and mostly it is sensors.
2. **Scaling buys resolution, not new operations**, more glomeruli, more
   ommatidia, more cortex doing low-level extraction.
3. **Counting neurons rather than grams dissolves the corvid puzzle** — and
   makes our own brain unnotable for its lineage.
4. **A system with no external reference cannot detect its own error**, the
   ant, stated once, and the whole of week 12.
