# Part 1 — Tolman's heresy

---

## The orthodoxy he was arguing with

By the 1940s the dominant account of maze learning was a **chain of
stimulus–response associations**: at this junction, turn left; at that smell,
go forward.

Learning was strengthening links. Nothing needed representing.

---

## Latent learning breaks it

Rats allowed to wander a maze **with no reward at all** later learn the rewarded
route faster than naive rats.

On an S–R account there was nothing to strengthen. No reward, no reinforcement,
no learning.

Something was acquired anyway.

---

## Place learning against response learning

Train a rat to food that is always in the **same place**, from varying start
points. It succeeds.

An S–R chain cannot transfer across start points, because the required response
differs from each one.

---

## Shortcuts

Block the trained route and open a set of novel radial arms.

Rats select the arm pointing at the **goal location**, a route they have never
run and never been rewarded for.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

Tolman, 1948: something map-like, not a chain of associations.

*Psychological Review* 55(4), 189–208.

# Part 2 — twenty-three years later, the cells

---

## O'Keefe & Dostrovsky, 1971

Recording in rat hippocampus, they find units that fire when the animal is in a
**particular place in the environment** — and not otherwise.

Not a response. Not a stimulus. A location.

*Brain Research* 34(1), 171–175.

---

## What place cells are not

- **Not** tied to a sensory cue, the field persists in darkness
- **Not** tied to a movement — it fires regardless of heading, in an open arena
- **Not** a single map, the population **remaps** across environments

O'Keefe & Nadel's *The Hippocampus as a Cognitive Map* (1978) is the synthesis.

---

## The rest of the toolkit

| Cell type | Codes |
| --- | --- |
| Place cells | where you are |
| **Grid cells** | a periodic metric over the whole surface |
| Head-direction cells | which way you face |
| Boundary vector cells | distance and bearing to walls |
| Speed cells | how fast you are going |

Enough, in principle, to do dead reckoning — which is what week 8's ant does
with vastly less.

---

## Hafting et al., 2005

Entorhinal cells whose firing fields tile the environment in a **regular
triangular lattice**.

Not one place. A periodic metric.

Nobel Prize 2014 — O'Keefe, and May-Britt and Edvard Moser.

---

# Why hexagonal?

---

## Because interference forces it

![Two panels showing the same hexagonally symmetric field of bright peaks on a dark ground. The left panel is labelled with spatial axes in metres and has a dashed hexagon marking the 60 degree symmetry. The right panel has identical structure but its axes are labelled as two abstract stimulus dimensions.](./figures/grid-cells.svg)

---

## The construction

```
field(x, y) = Σ cos(kᵢ · x)   for three wave vectors 60° apart
```

Threshold it, and a triangular lattice is the only thing that can come out.

So **60° symmetry is not an observation about rodents.** It is what summing
three plane waves at 60° does, anywhere, in any medium.

```notes
This is the slide that makes the 2016 result comprehensible rather than magical.
```

---

## Which gives the fMRI work its handle

You cannot resolve single grid cells in a human scanner.

But a population using a hexagonally symmetric code produces a **six-fold
periodic signal** as a function of movement direction, and that you can
measure.

# Part 3 — the reframe

---

## Constantinescu, O'Reilly & Behrens, 2016

Participants learn a space of **morphing bird silhouettes**: two continuous
dimensions, neck length and leg length.

No arena. No movement. No space.

They then "navigate" it — imagining transitions from one bird to another.

---

## The signal turns up anyway

**Hexagonally symmetric**, as a function of direction through the *conceptual*
space.

In entorhinal cortex and ventromedial prefrontal cortex.

**Stable across sessions acquired a week apart.**

*Science* 352(6292), 1464–1468.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

If one mechanism organises relational structure generally, space is just the instance we could see.

---

## Why we found it in space first

Not because space is special to the brain.

Because space is **observable to the experimenter**, you can put an animal in
a box and watch where it goes. You cannot watch it move through a space of bird
shapes.

The apparatus selected the domain, and we mistook that for the subject.

---

## The model that unifies them

Whittington et al., **the Tolman–Eichenbaum Machine**, *Cell* (2020).

One architecture in which spatial and relational memory are the same
computation, with place-like and grid-like codes emerging from the demand to
generalise structure across environments.

Named, deliberately, for the two people the field spent fifty years reconciling.

---

## What this does to the course

This week stops being a step toward cognition.

It becomes closer to the **substrate the later weeks run on** — if concepts are
navigated with the machinery of place, then the self-model of week 7, the system's
compressed representation of itself, and week 11's
categories inherit its properties.

Including its failure modes.

---

## The honest caveat

Whether the six-fold fMRI signal really reflects grid cells, or some other
periodically-tuned population, is **not settled**.

The inference runs: grid cells are hexagonal → this signal is hexagonal →
therefore grid cells. Which is an inference to the best available explanation,
not a measurement.

Grade **[C]**, and note that the 2016 result has replicated in other
conceptual domains, which strengthens it without closing it.

# Part 4 — and it lies

---

## A map buys you reach

Act on what you are not sensing. Plan a route you have not walked. Take a
shortcut.

Every one of those is unavailable to the system in week 4.

---

## What it costs

The map can be wrong in ways **the map cannot represent**.

Spatial memory is not a scale model with noise. It is systematically distorted,
in directions you can predict.

---

## Distortions that are reliably reproducible

- **Route-based inflation**, distance judged longer when the path between two
  points has more turns or intersections, even at equal metric distance
- **Boundary effects** — two points judged further apart when a boundary lies
  between them
- **Hierarchical error**, city A judged east of city B because *their regions*
  stand in that relation, when the cities do not
- **Asymmetry** — the distance from a landmark to an ordinary place is judged
  differently from the reverse

---

## The part that matters

None of those distortions is **available to the person making the judgement**.

There is no felt uncertainty attached to the wrong answer. The map reports a
number, and reports nothing about its own deformation.

---

## Which is the pattern, for the third time

| Week | System | What it cannot detect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | slime trail | whether the trail is its own or stale |
| 4 | association | cause against coincidence |
| **5** | **the map** | **its own distortion** |

Three mechanisms, three blind spots, and no internal signal in any of them.

---

## One comparative note before we leave

Menzel et al. (2005) displaced honeybees to unfamiliar release sites and
tracked them by harmonic radar.

They flew novel direct routes to the hive, behaviour that map-like memory
explains and vector memory alone does not. *PNAS* 102(8), 3040–3045.

A million neurons, doing something Tolman's rats were doubted for.
