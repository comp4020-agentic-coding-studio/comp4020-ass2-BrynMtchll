# Three programmes

---

## Not one

| Programme | The claim |
| --- | --- |
| Simon, 1955 — bounded rationality | optimising **under constraint** |
| Tversky & Kahneman, 1974 | systematic departures from normative models |
| Gigerenzer & Goldstein, 1996 | those departures are **well matched** to real environments |

---

## Start with the task everyone cites

**Wason's selection task** (1968). Four cards: **A**, **K**, **4**, **7**.

Rule: *if a card has a vowel on one side, it has an even number on the other.*

Which cards must you turn over?

---

## Most people get it wrong

The logically required answer is **A** and **7** — the vowel, and the odd
number that could falsify the rule.

Typically under 25% of participants select that pair. Most pick **A** and
**4**, which is confirmation rather than testing.

---

## And then Cosmides changed the content

Same logical structure, different subject matter: *if someone is drinking
alcohol, they must be over 18.* Cards showing a drink or an age.

Performance jumps dramatically, most people correctly check the beer drinker
and the underage person.

---

## Which is the whole debate in one pair of results

| Reading | What the content effect shows |
| --- | --- |
| Heuristics-and-biases | people fail at abstract logic and are helped by familiarity |
| Cosmides & Tooby | people were never running abstract logic — they run a **cheater-detection** procedure that the social version engages |
| Gigerenzer | the abstract version is an ill-posed question about an unfamiliar environment |

Cosmides, *Cognition* 31(3), 187–276 (1989).

---

## Simon was never a deficiency account

Satisficing is not failing.

It is what optimisation looks like when search is expensive.

---

## Gigerenzer's less-is-more

The recognition heuristic sometimes **outperforms** more information.

Take-the-best beats regression on some real datasets.

Simplicity as accuracy. Not as compromise.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

The popular version merged all three into "your brain is buggy". None of the three authors believes that.

---

## Simon was never a deficiency account

**Bounded rationality** (1955) is about optimising under constraint:
computation costs, search costs, information is incomplete.

*Satisficing* — take the first option above a threshold — is the rational
policy when search is expensive. It is not failing to optimise. It is
optimising including the cost of optimising.

---

## Which is a different claim from the next one

| Programme | Claim | Normative baseline |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simon, 1955 | agents optimise **under constraint** | constrained optimum |
| Tversky & Kahneman, 1974 | agents **depart systematically** from normative models | unconstrained optimum |
| Gigerenzer & Goldstein, 1996 | those departures are **well matched to real environments** | performance in the world |

The popular version merged all three into "your brain is buggy". None of the
three authors holds that.

---

## The third one makes a testable prediction

Gigerenzer's **less-is-more** claim: with limited data, a heuristic using one
cue can *outperform* a model using all of them, because complex models fit
noise.

So I tried to reproduce it.

---

## And it did not reproduce

![A line chart of out-of-sample accuracy against training set size on a log axis. The linear model using all eight cues outperforms take-the-best at every sample size tested, with no crossover. Titled as an attempted reproduction in which the effect did not appear.](./figures/less-is-more.svg)

Two environment structures tried. **Non-compensatory** means each cue outweighs
every later cue combined, which is the structure the claim is stated for.

No crossover in either.

---

## What that licenses, and what it does not

**It does not refute Gigerenzer & Goldstein.** Their demonstrations used a
specific real environment — German city populations — recognition rather than
validity-estimated take-the-best, and cue sets with known structure. An
abstract simulation is not the same test.

**It does suggest** the effect is more condition-dependent than its popular
statement implies, since a plausible reading of the stated conditions was not
sufficient to produce it.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

The honest conclusion is about my simulation, not about their claim. Knowing which is the skill.

# What did not hold

---

## The record this has to be read against

![Two panels. Left, Open Science Collaboration 2015: 97 of 100 original studies reported a significant result against 36 of 100 replications. Right, the ego depletion registered replication report across 23 laboratories, with the pooled estimate sitting at the null.](./figures/replication-record.svg)

OSC, *Science* 349(6251), aac4716 (2015).

---

## Ego depletion

23 laboratories. Preregistered. N = 2,141.

**No effect.**

Carter et al., 2015 had already challenged the resource account
meta-analytically.

---

## And it is not only psychology

- **Many Labs**: some classic effects replicate robustly across dozens of sites; others vanish entirely
- **Camerer et al.**: replications in experimental economics and social science, with success rates well below what the original literature implied
- Replication effect sizes average roughly **half** the originals, which matters even where statistical significance survives

The pattern is not "psychology is broken". It is that **published effect sizes
are systematically inflated**, for structural reasons rather than individual
ones.

---

## Why structural

Journals preferred novel positive results. Analytic flexibility was
undocumented and unpoliced. Direct replications were hard to publish, so nobody
was rewarded for running them.

Every incentive in that list is institutional. None requires anyone to behave
dishonestly — which is the same shape as *Stentor* in week 3 and Clever Hans in
week 4.

---

## And more

- social priming, largely collapsed
- the dual-process framing itself — contested (Melnikoff & Bargh, *The Mythical Number Two*)
- Open Science Collaboration, 2015, the background rate

---

## Kahneman, on his own book

Conceded that the priming chapter of *Thinking, Fast and Slow* rested on
literature that did not hold.

---

## What replaced them

The spec asks you to name three collapses *and* what stands in their place, so:

| Collapsed | What replaced it |
| --- | --- |
| Ego depletion as a limited resource | motivation and opportunity-cost accounts; attention allocation rather than fuel |
| Social priming as automatic behavioural control | much smaller, context-dependent effects; most of the striking demonstrations withdrawn |
| Two discrete systems | continuous accounts where "intuitive" and "deliberate" differ by degree, not kind |
| Bias catalogue as a list of defects | ecological rationality — performance judged against the environment rather than a norm |

Note that none of the replacements is "nothing". The phenomena did not vanish;
the **explanations** were too strong.

is subject to human error.

---

## Which applies to this course

Every grade on this site was assigned by someone with the same architecture

You have already seen one instance: I could not reproduce a claim I teach, and
the only reason you know is that I said so.

---

## So there is an obvious question

If the account is right, it applies to the account.

Next week does not escape that. It works out what follows from accepting it —
which is not scepticism, and not permission to believe anything.

---

## The move that should be standard

**Adversarial collaboration.** Mellers, Hertwig & Kahneman (2001) on the Linda
problem: two camps who disagreed designed a study together, in advance, with
agreed interpretations of each possible outcome.

Kahneman championed it. You meet it again next week, in consciousness research,
at field scale.

---

## Gigerenzer's "bias bias"

The field systematically **over-diagnoses** irrationality —

by comparing behaviour against norms that do not apply.

I think he is partly right. Tell me why he isn't.
