# The parallel that earns its place

---

## A swarm choosing a nest site

Scouts return. They dance for the site they found.

Several sites compete.

---

## Seeley et al., 2012

Scouts send inhibitory **stop signals**.

Specifically to bees dancing for **other** sites.

---

## That is cross inhibition

The same motif that operates between competing neural populations.

Doing the same job: **breaking deadlock** when evidence is close.

---

## Thirteen orders of magnitude

| Substrate | Mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| Neural populations | mutual inhibition between accumulators |
| Honeybee swarm | stop signals between scouts |

Not a loose analogy. The same solution.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

The strongest evidence on this course that we have been describing a class of solutions rather than a set of animals.

# Contested ground

---

## Clark & Chalmers, 1998

The notebook you rely on is **part of your cognitive system**.

---

## Adams & Aizawa reply

The coupling–constitution objection:

coupling a system to a resource does not make the resource **part of** it.

You need to be able to state both.

---

## And now two competent findings

That disagree.

---

## Lorenz et al., 2011

Mild social influence cut the **diversity** of estimates.

Without cutting the collective **error**.

Groups became more confident and no more accurate.

---

## Becker, Brackbill & Centola, 2017

In **decentralised** network structures, influence *improves* accuracy.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

The disagreement is about network topology, not about people.
