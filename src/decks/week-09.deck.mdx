---

## And the arms act on it

A severed octopus arm will still **complete motor programmes** — reaching,
grasping, passing an object along itself.

The axial nerve cord generates and terminates the behaviour locally. Central
command is not issuing the movement; at most it is requesting one.

---

## Which makes the vertebrate question malformed, not merely inapplicable

"What does the central controller represent?" presupposes a central controller
doing the representing.

In an animal where most of the nervous system is distributed and locally
autonomous, that question imports its answer before the investigation starts.

---

## A forward link worth making

An octopus is not a colony. But the control problem it solves, coordinating
semi-autonomous units holding local information — is closer to a swarm's than
to ours.

Same problem, different substrate. Next week, in bees.

# Nagel, 1974

---

## What is it like to be a bat?

Four words that have done more work in philosophy of mind than almost any
others.

The claim: there is something it is like, and we have no access to it.

---

## Akins' objection

The question may presuppose something false —

that bat sensory systems are **representational** in the way the question
requires.

---

## Why that matters enormously

| If | Then our failure to answer |
| --- | --- |
| The question is well-formed | tells us about the limits of imagination |
| The question is malformed | tells us about **the question** |

---

## I have no settled view

The seminar is genuinely open.

This is one of two weeks where I argue against my own lecture.

---

## The disjunction under the question

![A diagram: the question what is it like to be a bat presupposes that bat sensory states are representational in the way the question requires. If that holds, our failure to answer tells us about the limits of imagination. If it fails, our failure tells us about the question.](./figures/nagel-akins.svg)

---

## Why the disjunction matters more than either branch

In both cases we cannot answer.

On the first branch that is a fact about **us**, the limits of what can be
imagined from inside one sensory life.

On the second it is a fact about **the question**, which was built on a premise
about bats that may be false.

Same inability. Opposite conclusions. And introspection cannot tell you which.

---

## Akins' argument, stated rather than gestured at

Bat sensory systems may be better described as organised around **action** —
tracking what matters for the next wingbeat — than as building a representation
of how the world is.

If there is no world-model of the relevant kind in there, there may be no "what
it is like" of the relevant kind to fail to imagine.

Grade **[C]**. This is genuinely open and I do not have a settled view.

# A test of the account

---

## Cuttlefish and delayed gratification

Schnell et al. (2021, *Proc. R. Soc. B*): cuttlefish forgo an immediately
available but less-preferred prey item to wait for a preferred one.

Waiting up to roughly 130 seconds, and individual performance correlated with
learning performance in a separate reversal-learning task.

---

## Why that result belongs in weeks 8–10

Delayed gratification is usually framed as requiring future-oriented control,
and discussed almost entirely in primates and corvids.

A cephalopod arriving at it independently is exactly the convergence evidence
these three weeks exist to collect.

**[C]** — one paradigm, modest samples, and the inference from waiting to
self-control is the contestable step.

---

## And a question with legal consequences

Birch and colleagues' 2021 review for the UK government assessed evidence for
sentience across invertebrate groups.

Cephalopods and decapods were judged to have **strong enough** evidence, and
both were subsequently brought into UK animal welfare legislation.

---

## Note what happened there

A question this course treats as unresolved was **operationalised into law**,
because a decision had to be made either way.

Suspending judgement is a luxury of the seminar room. Regulators do not have
it, and they made the call on the evidence available.

```notes
Ask what threshold they would have advised, and what it would cost to be wrong
in each direction.
```
