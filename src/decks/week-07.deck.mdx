# Part 1 — a constraint before any evidence

---

## The compression is not optional

![A diagram: the whole system, with billions of parameters running continuously and in parallel, compressed by necessity into a low-dimensional serial self-model, which is what introspection returns. Everything the compression dropped has no channel to the report.](./figures/self-model-compression.svg)

---

## Why it has to be lossy

A complete model of a process cannot run **inside** that process at full
resolution. The model would need at least as much capacity as the thing modelled,
and it is a part of it.

So the self-model is a summary. That much is structural, and follows from week 1's
constraint rather than from any experiment.

---

## Put a number on the compression

A conservative accounting of what a self-model would have to cover:

```
cortical neurons                    ≈  1.6 × 10^10
synapses                            ≈  1.0 × 10^14
```

And what a report can carry:

```
speech, sustained                   ≈  40 bits/second
a 2-minute answer                   ≈  4,800 bits
```

---

## The ratio is the point

```
10^14 synaptic states  →  ~5 × 10^3 bits of report
```

The compression is not lossy at the margins. It discards essentially
everything, and what survives was selected by something you also cannot
inspect.

**Sanity check:** the exact numbers do not matter. Change them by four orders
of magnitude in either direction and the conclusion is unchanged.

---

## The moral

"Introspection is a summary" sounds like a caution about accuracy.

Put the ratio in and it is stronger than that: the report and the process are
not the same *kind* of object, and no amount of care in reporting closes a gap
of ten orders of magnitude.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

And a summary does not carry a note saying what it left out.

---

## What that predicts

If introspection reads a summary, then introspective reports should be:

- **fluent**, the summary is compact and easy to read
- **confident** — nothing in it flags its own incompleteness
- **wrong in specific ways**, wherever the dropped detail mattered
- **not correctable by effort** — you cannot introspect harder onto missing data

All four are testable. The last three have been tested.

---

## Metzinger's version

*Being No One* (2003): the self is a **phenomenal self-model**, a
representation the system builds and then fails to recognise as a
representation.

The philosophical claim is strong and contested. The empirical work below is
more unsettling than the philosophy, which is unusual.

# Part 2 — Nisbett & Wilson, 1977

---

## Telling more than we can know

A review, not an experiment, and still one of the most cited papers in the
field. *Psychological Review* 84(3), 231–259.

Their survey: across many studies, people asked why they behaved as they did
produced confident accounts that **did not match the manipulations actually
driving the behaviour**.

---

## Their explanation

Reports are not read off the process.

They are **generated from plausible theories** about what would cause such
behaviour — the same theories an observer would use.

Which predicts something specific: an observer with the same background
knowledge should be about as accurate as the actor. Often they are.

# Part 3 — the experiment that makes it vivid

---

## The design

![A flow diagram: two face photographs are presented, the participant chooses the more attractive, the experimenter covertly hands back the other one, and then asks why they chose it. Fewer than 20% detect the swap; over 80% explain the choice citing features of the face they had rejected.](./figures/choice-blindness-protocol.svg)

Johansson, Hall, Sikström & Olsson, *Science* 310(5745), 116–119 (2005).

---

## The numbers

![A horizontal bar chart. Eighty-four percent said in advance that they would notice a swap. No more than twenty percent of manipulations were detected by the end of a session, and fewer than ten percent were easily spotted. The gap between prediction and performance is marked.](./figures/choice-blindness.svg)

---

## And then they explained it

Not "I don't know". Not hesitation.

Participants gave reasons — **citing features unique to the face they had
actually turned down**. The blonde hair they had rejected became the reason
they chose her.

---

## Why this is worse than an error

An error is a wrong output.

This is a **confabulation**: the reporting system did its job, produced fluent
content, and the content was manufactured. There was no felt difference between
that and a true report.

Which is exactly what a summary-reader with no completeness flag should do.

# Part 4 — the objection

---

## Petitmengin et al., 2013

You are required to know this, and most summaries of choice blindness omit it.

When participants are given **space to examine their experience**, an
interview technique that asks them to attend to what actually happened, rather
than pressure to produce an explanation —

the effect **largely dissolves**. Recognition of the swap, sometimes up to 45
minutes later.

---

## So grade it properly

| Claim | Grade |
| --- | --- |
| Introspection is simply unreliable | **[C]** — does not survive Petitmengin |
| Introspection is unreliable *under demand to explain* | much better supported |

---

## The weaker claim is the more interesting one

Because the conditions under which we normally deploy introspection are
**precisely** the conditions where it fails: being asked to account for
ourselves, quickly, to someone who expects an answer.

Interviews. Exams. Arguments. Therapy. Seminars.

```notes
This is the point to let sit. The failure is situational, and the situation is
the one we live in.
```

# Part 5 — an assay under too much load

---

## The mark test

Gallup, 1970: anaesthetise a chimpanzee, apply a dye mark where it cannot be
seen without a mirror, and see whether the animal inspects the mark on itself.

*Science* 167(3914), 86–87. For fifty years, the standard operationalisation of
self-recognition.

---

## Kohda et al., 2019

**Cleaner wrasse pass it.** *Labroides dimidiatus* — a fish — showed throat
scraping against surfaces after a brown mark was applied to its throat, visible
only in the mirror.

*PLOS Biology* 17(2), e3000021.

---

## De Waal's commentary, same issue

Three objections, and they are methodological rather than dismissive:

1. MSR requires that **social** responses to the mirror have been replaced by self-directed behaviour first
2. The mark should be **purely visual** — the brown throat mark may have been physically irritating
3. No prior training

His verdict: an intermediate level of mirror understanding, closer to monkeys
than hominids. *PLOS Biology* 17(2), e3000112.

---

## And Gallup objected too

The inventor of the test argued against the interpretation. Sample size was four
fish. The scraping could reflect an attempt to remove an irritant rather than
self-inspection.

---

## Kohda et al., 2022

They returned with more.

**17 of 18 cleaner fish** — 94% — showed the same behaviour. The largest sample
tested for MSR in any non-human species outside chimpanzees, with the highest
pass rate at that sample size.

*PLOS Biology* 20(2), e3001529.

---

## We are not going to settle it

And the reason it is on the course is not the fish.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

The argument is about what the test was ever a test of.

---

## Unpack that

The mark test measures: *does the animal use mirror-reflected information to
direct behaviour at its own body?*

The inference runs from that to **self-awareness**, which is a much larger
claim, and the inference was never licensed by the assay.

A behavioural test carrying a philosophical conclusion is the structure to
notice here, you will meet it again in week 12 with integrated information
theory.

---

## A useful counter-critique

A commentary in *Learning & Behavior* argued the reaction to Kohda's result
revealed more about **scientists' priors about phylogeny** than about
self-awareness.

Conclusions about a species should rest on the whole corpus rather than one
test and an expectation about where in the tree the animal sits.

Which is a Clever Hans point about the experimenters, from week 4.

# What week 7 established

---

## Four things

1. The self-model is **necessarily** a summary — that is structural, not empirical.
2. Reports are **generated**, not read off, and the generator is fluent.
3. The unreliability is **situational**, and the situation is the ordinary one.
4. An assay can be asked to carry a conclusion it never licensed.
