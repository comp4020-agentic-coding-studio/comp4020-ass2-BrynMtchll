From here to week 10 the course stops adding and starts testing. If the account
built over seven weeks only describes vertebrates, it was never an account of
intelligence, and these are the weeks where we find out.

## The puzzle, and why it was the wrong puzzle

Corvids and parrots do things comparable to great apes with brains a fraction of
the size, which looked like a deep problem for any information-processing view.
Olkowicz and colleagues measured it properly in 2016 across 28 species: parrot
and songbird brains carry roughly **twice** the neurons of primate brains of
equal mass, and a higher proportion sits in the forebrain. Large parrots and
corvids match or exceed monkeys with far bigger brains on forebrain neuron count.

The puzzle largely dissolves. They were never small in the currency that matters.

Herculano-Houzel, a co-author there, runs the complementary argument about us:
the human brain is an ordinary primate brain scaled up, with no special
encephalisation. What's unusual is the absolute cortical neuron count and the
metabolic budget required to run it. Chittka and Niven's 2009 review makes the
insect case, that differentiated behaviour and something like categorisation need
far fewer neurons than anyone assumed.

## The ant

Then the best experiment on the course.

*Cataglyphis fortis* navigates by path integration — direction from polarised
skylight, distance from counting its own strides. Wittlinger, Wehner and Wolf
tested the distance half in 2006 by gluing pig-bristle stilts to the ants' legs
after the outbound run, or trimming their legs to stumps. Stilted ants overshot
the nest. Stumped ants undershot. Ants altered *before* the outbound run
navigated correctly, having counted the same stride in both directions.

What the ant stores is a vector, not a path. So it always knows where home is
and never knows how it got where it is, and the integrator has no independent
reference against which to check itself. An ant that is lost behaves exactly like
an ant that is not. Hold that sentence until week 12.

![Two panels. Left, a simulated wandering outbound path from the nest, with the true direction home and the slightly wrong homeward vector the ant actually runs, missing by a couple of body lengths. Right, mean miss distance against outbound path length, crossing the width of the nest entrance at around fifty steps.](../../decks/figures/path-integration-error.svg)

*Simulated path integration. The integrator is good — errors partly cancel on a wandering path — and it is still not good enough, because the target is a hole. Past roughly fifty steps the expected miss exceeds the nest entrance, which is why a returning ant spirals.*

## Two things with no obvious function

Bumblebees roll wooden balls with no reward attached, detouring to do it, younger
ones more than older — which meets behavioural criteria for play. Jumping
spiders, observed as unpigmented spiderlings whose retinal tubes are visible
through the cuticle, show regular bouts of retinal movement with limb twitching
that lengthen through the night, which looks a great deal like REM sleep.

Neither result tells us what we want it to. Both are graded <abbr class="grade grade-c" title="contested">[C]</abbr>, and the
seminar is about what evidence would move either one.
