A system that models its environment can model itself, and the moment it does
there's a constraint waiting: the self-model has to be smaller than the system.
A complete model of a process, running inside that process, is not available. So
what introspection reports is a summary — and summaries omit, without marking
what they omitted.

Metzinger's *Being No One* is the ambitious philosophical version. The empirical
work is more unsettling than the philosophy.

## Choice blindness

Johansson and colleagues had people choose the more attractive of two faces, then
covertly handed them the one they had rejected and asked why they picked it.
Fewer than one in ten manipulations were caught. Under 20% across a whole
session. People then explained their choice, citing features specific to the face
they had actually turned down.

Beforehand, **84% said they would notice.**

The objection, which you should arrive knowing, is Petitmengin and colleagues'
2013 result: when participants are given room to examine their experience rather
than pressure to produce an explanation, the effect largely dissolves, sometimes
with recognition up to 45 minutes later. So the strong reading — introspection is
simply unreliable — does not survive. Grade the strong version **[C]**, and note
that the weaker version is more interesting anyway: introspection is unreliable
*under the conditions where we usually deploy it*, which is while being asked to
account for ourselves.

## The mirror test, as a lesson in method

Kohda and colleagues reported in 2019 that cleaner wrasse pass the mark test.
De Waal's commentary in the same journal argued the marks were physically
irritating and the behaviour ambiguous, so the result falls short of mirror
self-recognition. Gallup, who devised the test, objected. Kohda's group returned
in 2022 with eighteen fish and a 94% pass rate.

We are not going to settle it. The reason it's here is that it shows a
behavioural assay carrying more interpretive weight than it can bear, and the
argument is really about what the test was ever a test *of*. Which is a
philosophical question wearing a methods section.
