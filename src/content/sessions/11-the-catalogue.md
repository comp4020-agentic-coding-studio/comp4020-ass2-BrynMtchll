This is the week the course is named after, and it is not the week where I list
the ways you are stupid.

## Three programmes, not one

Simon's bounded rationality, from 1955, is about optimising under constraint. It
is not a deficiency account and never was. Tversky and Kahneman's 1974 programme
catalogued systematic departures from normative models, which is a different
claim. Gigerenzer and Goldstein's 1996 work on fast and frugal heuristics argues
those departures are well matched to real environments and only look broken in
artificial tasks.

Getting these three apart is most of the week's work, because the popular version
has merged them into "your brain is buggy", which none of the three authors
believes.

## What did not hold

Then the uncomfortable part, and the reason this course can't simply teach the
canon.

Ego depletion: a 23-laboratory preregistered replication with 2,141 participants
found no effect, and Carter and colleagues' meta-analytic work had already
challenged the resource account. Social priming: largely collapsed. The Open
Science Collaboration's 2015 estimate of reproducibility across psychology is the
background against which all of this should be read. Kahneman himself conceded
that the priming chapter of *Thinking, Fast and Slow* rested on literature that
did not hold.

And the dual-process story that organises most undergraduate teaching of this
material is itself contested — Melnikoff and Bargh's "The Mythical Number Two"
is the reading.

## The point

A course called *How and Why You're Dumb* that recited this canon uncritically
would be doing the exact thing it claims to be about: accepting a satisfying
story because it was satisfying. The literature on human error is subject to
human error, and there is no vantage point outside that.

Gigerenzer's "bias bias" — the argument that the field systematically
over-diagnoses irrationality by comparing behaviour against norms that don't
apply — is the sharpest version, and I think he is partly right. You are welcome
to tell me why he isn't.

Your revision is due this week. It should be visibly harder to write after this
seminar than before it.
