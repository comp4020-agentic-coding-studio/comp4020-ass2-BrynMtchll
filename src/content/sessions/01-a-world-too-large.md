Bring a plain white card and a pen. That is the whole equipment list for the
semester.

## The constraint

We start with something that is not a fact about brains but a fact about
systems. A finite thing embedded in a world larger than itself cannot represent
that world; it can only represent some compression of it, and every compression
throws information away. Ashby put the formal version in 1956 — a regulator
needs at least as much variety as the disturbances it has to counter — and
Shannon had already given us the mathematics of what it costs to throw things
away on purpose.

This is worth getting straight before any biology arrives, because it means the
interesting question is never "how much intelligence does this animal have". It
is "what did this system decide not to represent, and what did that buy it".

## The card

Twenty minutes of the seminar goes on the oldest demonstration in the course.
You will map the blind spot in your own eye, the patch of retina where the optic
nerve leaves and there are no photoreceptors at all. It is roughly the size of
nine full moons. You have had it your entire life and have never once seen a
hole.

What you are looking at when the dot disappears is not the gap. It's the filling
— your visual system has been generating plausible content across that region
continuously, without flagging it, and you had no way to know. Hold onto the
structure of that, because in week 12 we come back to it with a harder version:
the blind spot you can't find with a card.

## The grading scheme

The rest of the session sets up the notation you will use all semester. Every
substantive claim on this site carries one of three grades — **[E]** for
established, **[C]** for contested, **[S]** for speculative — and by week 3 you
will be assigning them yourself.

We practise on four claims, two of which are wrong in ways that took the field a
long time to notice. Nobody gets all four right, and the point of doing it in
week 1 rather than week 10 is that you find out early that "sounds right" and
"is supported" are different properties of a sentence.

## What you leave with

The reading for week 2, and the brief for the position statement due at the end
of next week. Write that before you do the reading, not after. It is the only
piece of work in this course that is better for being uninformed.
