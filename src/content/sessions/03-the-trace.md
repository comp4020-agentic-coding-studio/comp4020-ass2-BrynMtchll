*Physarum polycephalum* has no neurons and solves the U-shaped trap problem,
which is a standard test in robot navigation. It does this by avoiding its own
slime trail. The memory is real and it is not inside the organism — it's smeared
on the substrate, and the organism reads it the way it reads anything else in
the environment.

Reid and colleagues showed in 2012 that disguising the trail breaks the
behaviour, which is what makes this a claim about memory rather than a story
about mould. Note what the architecture buys: unlimited capacity, no maintenance
cost, and total vulnerability to anyone with a pipette.

## The part of this week that matters most

In 1906 Jennings described a hierarchy of avoidance responses in the ciliate
*Stentor roeseli* — bend away, alter the cilia, contract, detach — escalating
under repeated stimulation. One cell, running something that looks like a
decision procedure.

During the behaviourist era it was discredited as non-reproducible, and it stayed
discredited for most of a century. Dexter, Prabakaran and Gunawardena replicated
it in 2019 and found why the replications had failed: **they used the wrong
species.** *S. coeruleus* is motile and builds no tube. *S. roeseli* is sessile.
The two animals do not face the same problem, so of course they don't behave the
same way.

Sit with that, because it is not a story about fraud or about statistics. A
correct finding was thrown away for ninety years because nobody checked a species
name, and everyone involved was behaving reasonably. This is the failure mode
this course is actually about, and it is much harder to guard against than
dishonesty.

## Two plant literatures, graded differently

We finish on plants, where the evidence splits cleanly and instructively.

Gagliano and colleagues reported associative learning in pea plants in 2016.
Markel failed to replicate it in 2020, using blinded scoring. Gagliano commented
that the protocol was unsuitable; Markel responded that the differences could not
account for the failure. Both exchanges are published, neither party conceded,
and the matter is open. Grade: **[C]**.

Dener, Kacelnik and Shemesh, also 2016, split pea root systems between a
constant and a variable nutrient patch with the same mean. Plants grew more root
into the variable patch when nutrients were low and less when they were high —
risk-prone when poor, risk-averse when rich, which is the energy budget rule out
of animal foraging theory. This one has held up. Grade: **[E]**.

Two papers, same year, same plant, same broad claim family, and they do not get
the same grade. You will be asked to explain why in the seminar, and "one
replicated" is the beginning of the answer rather than the whole of it.
