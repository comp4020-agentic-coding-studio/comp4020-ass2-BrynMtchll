Olkowicz and colleagues (2016) measured neuron numbers across 28 bird species: parrot and songbird brains carry around twice the neurons of equal-mass primate brains, concentrated in the forebrain. Corvids and parrots were never small in the currency that matters. Herculano-Houzel's parallel argument is that our own brain is an ordinary primate brain scaled up.

Then the ant. Wittlinger, Wehner and Wolf (2006) put *Cataglyphis* on stilts and on stumps and showed distance is measured by stride integration. What the animal stores is a vector rather than a route, so it cannot audit its own error, and a lost ant is behaviourally identical to one that is not.

That sentence is the one I want you carrying into week 12.
