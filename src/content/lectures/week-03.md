*Physarum* navigates by refusing to cross its own slime, which puts its memory on the substrate rather than inside it. Reid and colleagues (2012) established this by disguising the trail.

The bulk of the lecture is the *Stentor* case. Jennings described a behavioural hierarchy in one cell in 1906; the behaviourist era discarded it as irreproducible; Dexter and colleagues replicated it in 2019 and found the failed replications had used a different species with a different mode of life. A correct result was thrown away for most of a century because nobody checked a name.

I use this rather than a fraud case on purpose. Everyone involved behaved reasonably.
