Ten quizzes, one most weeks, ten to fifteen minutes each. Your best eight count.

They are marked with **certainty-based marking**, which is not a gimmick and not
invented for this course. Gardner-Medwin developed it at UCL, where it has run
across seventeen summative medical examinations.

## How the scoring works

With each answer you declare a certainty level. What the answer is worth depends
on that declaration.

| Declared certainty | If correct | If wrong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low | +1 | 0 |
| Mid | +2 | −2 |
| High | +3 | −6 |

So a confident error costs you six marks, and a hedged error costs you nothing.

## Why you cannot game it

This is a **proper scoring rule**. That is a technical property, and it means
reporting your actual confidence maximises your expected score — there is no
clever strategy that beats honesty. The thresholds work out at roughly 67% for
mid and 80% for high: below those, declaring high is a bad bet even though it
pays more when you are right.

Which removes the dilemma that ordinary negative marking creates, where you have
to decide whether answering at all is worth the risk. Here the question is never
whether to answer. It is only how much to stake.

## What to expect in the first fortnight

Your score will look bad, and this is normal rather than a sign that you are
doing badly.

Students who answer 80% of questions correctly typically collect around half the
available marks. The gap between those two numbers is not a scoring quirk. It's
the difference between how right you were and how right you thought you were —
which is the subject of week 12, arriving early and in your own results.

Most people misuse the scale for two weeks and then it clicks. Tuyet runs a
practice quiz in week 1 that does not count, and it is worth doing.

## A note on reading

Certainty-based marking puts a premium on having *reasons* to be sure. Answers
memorised from a summary tend to attract high confidence without the reasons
that would justify it, and the scoring punishes exactly that. Reading the papers
is the cheaper path.
