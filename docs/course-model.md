# The course model — research consolidation

Working document. Not site content. This is the artefact the outline is derived
from, per the research-before-outline rule in `CLAUDE.md`.

## Thesis

**Intelligence is lossy compression of a world too large to represent, and every
compression has a shape you cannot see from inside it.**

Three consequences the course has to earn, not assert:

1. Every capacity that buys predictive power does so by discarding something.
2. The discarded part is invisible from inside the system that discarded it.
3. Therefore no model — including this one, and including the science of
   models — is assumption-free. Models are comparable by track record, not by
   correspondence to an unmediated world.

This is **fallibilism, not relativism.** "All models rest on assumptions" does
not license "all models are equal". If a week drifts toward the second, it is
wrong.

## Why this is not a ladder

The build ascends in *capacity dependency*, not in *evolutionary rank*. There is
no scala naturae here: a nematode is not a draft of a human. Later weeks depend
on earlier capacities logically — you cannot plan without a forward model — and
that is the only sense in which anything is "higher".

The branch weeks (8–10) exist to enforce this. They are the **control group**:
lineages that compressed the same world differently, and whose bounds are
visible to us precisely because we are outside them. That asymmetry is the
course's closing move.

## The unit of a week

Every week declares four things, and the site's own spec tests enforce the first
three:

- **capacity** — what the model can now do
- **depends on** — the capacities it requires (must already be introduced)
- **approximation** — the shortcut that buys it
- **gap** — what the shortcut makes invisible

A week that adds a capacity with no named approximation and no named gap is a
defect: it has broken the thesis.

## Evidence grading

Every substantive claim on the site carries one of three registers, and they may
never blur inside a paragraph:

- **[E] established** — replicated, or uncontested primary evidence
- **[C] contested** — live disagreement in the field; the disagreement is the
  content, and both sides get named
- **[S] speculative** — reasoned extension beyond evidence, labelled as such

The contested set is maintained deliberately, because the failure mode for a
course with this title is reciting a satisfying canon that has not held up.
Standing members: ego depletion, social priming, the Dunning-Kruger curve,
plant associative learning, mirror self-recognition in fish, IIT's testability.

# Outline

## Part I — The premise (weeks 1–2)

### Week 1 — A world too large
**Capacity:** none yet; the frame. **Approximation:** that "intelligence" can be
defined at all. **Gap:** every definition smuggles in a criterion.

Sets up compression as the organising idea, and the Umwelt as the claim that an
organism's world *is* its sensory bandwidth. Establishes the evidence-grading
scheme students use all semester.

- Shannon, C. E. (1948). A mathematical theory of communication. *Bell System
  Technical Journal*, 27, 379–423, 623–656.
- von Uexküll, J. (1934/2010). *A Foray into the Worlds of Animals and Humans*.
  University of Minnesota Press.
- Dennett, D. (1987). *The Intentional Stance*. MIT Press. — on when
  attributing cognition is licensed.
- Godfrey-Smith, P. (2016). *Other Minds*. Farrar, Straus and Giroux. ch. 1–2.

### Week 2 — Measurement and valuation
**Capacity:** sense a gradient; collapse it to better/worse. **Depends on:**
nothing. **Approximation:** a multidimensional world reduced to one scalar.
**Gap:** no representation of *why* anything is good.

*E. coli* chemotaxis as the minimal case: run-and-tumble, a memory of
milliseconds, no representation at all.

- Berg, H. C. (2004). *E. coli in Motion*. Springer.
- Macnab, R. M., & Koshland, D. E. (1972). The gradient-sensing mechanism in
  bacterial chemotaxis. *PNAS*, 69(9), 2509–2512.

## Part II — Interior models (weeks 3–7)

### Week 3 — The trace: memory without a brain
**Capacity:** the past constrains the present. **Approximation:** history
compressed into a single trace. **Gap:** a trace cannot distinguish causes.

*Physarum* externalises its memory into slime — memory as environmental
modification rather than internal state. Paired with the pea-plant learning
dispute as the semester's first worked example of grading contested evidence.

- Reid, C. R., Latty, T., Dussutour, A., & Beekman, M. (2012). Slime mould uses
  an externalized spatial "memory" to navigate in complex environments. *PNAS*,
  109(43), 17490–17494. doi:10.1073/pnas.1215037109 **[E]**
- Nakagaki, T., Yamada, H., & Tóth, Á. (2000). Maze-solving by an amoeboid
  organism. *Nature*, 407, 470. **[E]**
- Gagliano, M., Vyazovskiy, V. V., Borbély, A. A., Grimonprez, M., &
  Depczynski, M. (2016). Learning by association in plants. *Scientific
  Reports*, 6, 38427. doi:10.1038/srep38427 **[C]**
- Markel, K. (2020). Lack of evidence for associative learning in pea plants.
  *eLife*, 9, e57614. doi:10.7554/eLife.57614 **[C]**
- Gagliano et al. (2020). Comment on 'Lack of evidence for associative learning
  in pea plants'. *eLife*, 9, e61141.
- Markel, K. (2020). Response to comment. *eLife*, 9, e61689.

### Week 4 — Association: correlation standing in for cause
**Capacity:** link events. **Depends on:** trace, valuation. **Approximation:**
co-occurrence treated as connection. **Gap:** superstition — the mechanism
cannot tell a real cause from a reliable coincidence.

- Kandel, E. R. (2001). The molecular biology of memory storage: a dialogue
  between genes and synapses. *Science*, 294(5544), 1030–1038.
- Rescorla, R. A., & Wagner, A. R. (1972). A theory of Pavlovian conditioning.
  In *Classical Conditioning II*. Appleton-Century-Crofts.
- Pfungst, O. (1911). *Clever Hans (The Horse of Mr. von Osten)*. Holt. — the
  founding lesson in experimenter artefact.

### Week 5 — The map: compressing space
**Capacity:** represent structure not currently sensed. **Depends on:**
association. **Approximation:** space as graph or metric. **Gap:** systematic,
predictable distortion — the map is not the territory.

- Tolman, E. C. (1948). Cognitive maps in rats and men. *Psychological Review*,
  55(4), 189–208.
- O'Keefe, J., & Dostrovsky, J. (1971). The hippocampus as a spatial map.
  *Brain Research*, 34(1), 171–175.
- Menzel, R., et al. (2005). Honey bees navigate according to a map-like spatial
  memory. *PNAS*, 102(8), 3040–3045.

### Week 6 — Prediction: perception as inference
**Capacity:** generate the world before sensing it. **Depends on:** map.
**Approximation:** perception is mostly top-down construction. **Gap:** you
experience your prediction, not the world. The master gap of the course.

- Clark, A. (2013). Whatever next? Predictive brains, situated agents, and the
  future of cognitive science. *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 36(3), 181–204.
- Friston, K. (2010). The free-energy principle: a unified brain theory?
  *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 11(2), 127–138. **[C]** — unifying framework
  to some, unfalsifiable to others; say so.
- Seth, A. (2021). *Being You: A New Science of Consciousness*. Faber.
- Simons, D. J., & Chabris, C. F. (1999). Gorillas in our midst. *Perception*,
  28(9), 1059–1074.

### Week 7 — The self-model
**Capacity:** model the modeller. **Depends on:** prediction. **Approximation:**
the self as a low-dimensional summary. **Gap:** introspection reports the
summary, not the process — so the system cannot audit itself.

The mirror-test literature runs here as a method critique: what a behavioural
assay can and cannot license.

- Metzinger, T. (2003). *Being No One*. MIT Press.
- Gallup, G. G. (1970). Chimpanzees: self-recognition. *Science*, 167(3914),
  86–87.
- Kohda, M., et al. (2019). If a fish can pass the mark test, what are the
  implications for consciousness and self-awareness testing in animals? *PLOS
  Biology*, 17(2), e3000021. **[C]**
- de Waal, F. B. M. (2019). Fish, mirrors, and a gradualist perspective on
  self-awareness. *PLOS Biology*, 17(2), e3000112. **[C]**
- Kohda, M., et al. (2022). Further evidence for the capacity of mirror
  self-recognition in cleaner fish. *PLOS Biology*, 20(2), e3001529. **[C]**
- Nisbett, R. E., & Wilson, T. D. (1977). Telling more than we can know.
  *Psychological Review*, 84(3), 231–259.

## Part III — The control group (weeks 8–10)

These weeks exist to establish the asymmetry the course closes on: **a bound is
visible from outside and invisible from inside.**

### Week 8 — Small brains, full repertoires
**Convergence evidence:** capacities from Part II implemented on ~10⁶ neurons.
**Gap visible from outside:** we can state the bee's bounds precisely.

- Chittka, L., & Niven, J. (2009). Are bigger brains better? *Current Biology*,
  19(21), R995–R1008. doi:10.1016/j.cub.2009.08.023
- Howard, S. R., Avarguès-Weber, A., Garcia, J. E., Greentree, A. D., & Dyer,
  A. G. (2018). Numerical ordering of zero in honey bees. *Science*, 360(6393),
  1124–1126. doi:10.1126/science.aar4975 **[C]** — replication and
  task-artefact questions are live.
- Jackson, R. R., & Cross, F. R. (2011). Spider cognition. *Advances in Insect
  Physiology*, 41, 115–174.

### Week 9 — Alien architecture
**Convergence evidence:** a differently-organised nervous system, largely
outside the brain. Nagel's question stops being rhetorical.

- Godfrey-Smith, P. (2016). *Other Minds*. FSG.
- Nagel, T. (1974). What is it like to be a bat? *The Philosophical Review*,
  83(4), 435–450.
- Hochner, B. (2012). An embodied view of octopus neurobiology. *Current
  Biology*, 22(20), R887–R892.

### Week 10 — Offloaded and collective compression
**Capacity:** externalise the model — into the environment, the group, the
artefact. **Approximation:** trust a process you do not hold. **Gap:** the group
has blind spots no member can see, and they are not the sum of individual ones.

- Clark, A., & Chalmers, D. (1998). The extended mind. *Analysis*, 58(1), 7–19.
- Seeley, T. D. (2010). *Honeybee Democracy*. Princeton University Press.
- Hutchins, E. (1995). *Cognition in the Wild*. MIT Press.
- Bikhchandani, S., Hirshleifer, D., & Welch, I. (1992). A theory of fads,
  fashion, custom, and cultural change as informational cascades. *Journal of
  Political Economy*, 100(5), 992–1026.

## Part IV — The human case and its limits (weeks 11–12)

### Week 11 — The catalogue, and the catalogue's own problem
**Capacity:** language and symbolic compression; heuristics tuned to
environments. **Approximation:** discrete tokens for continuous states; fast
rules for expensive inference. **Gap:** what tokens cannot carry, and rules
misfiring outside the environment that shaped them.

The week deliberately turns on itself: much of the canonical "you are
irrational" literature has not replicated. A course with this title that recited
it uncritically would be committing its own subject.

- Simon, H. A. (1955). A behavioral model of rational choice. *Quarterly Journal
  of Economics*, 69(1), 99–118.
- Tversky, A., & Kahneman, D. (1974). Judgment under uncertainty: heuristics and
  biases. *Science*, 185(4157), 1124–1131.
- Gigerenzer, G., & Goldstein, D. G. (1996). Reasoning the fast and frugal way.
  *Psychological Review*, 103(4), 650–669. — the ecological-rationality
  counterweight: heuristics as well-matched, not broken.
- Wason, P. C. (1968). Reasoning about a rule. *Quarterly Journal of
  Experimental Psychology*, 20(3), 273–281.
- Cosmides, L. (1989). The logic of social exchange. *Cognition*, 31(3),
  187–276.
- Open Science Collaboration (2015). Estimating the reproducibility of
  psychological science. *Science*, 349(6251), aac4716.
- Hagger, M. S., et al. (2016). A multilab preregistered replication of the
  ego-depletion effect. *Perspectives on Psychological Science*, 11(4),
  546–573. doi:10.1177/1745691616652873 **[C]**
- Carter, E. C., Kofler, L. M., Forster, D. E., & McCullough, M. E. (2015). A
  series of meta-analytic tests of the depletion effect. *JEP: General*, 144,
  796–815.

### Week 12 — The blind spot
**Capacity:** none added. The model is turned on itself. **Gap:** structural and
unclosable.

Three moves, in order:

1. **The metacognitive gap, carefully.** Dunning-Kruger is the obvious
   punchline, and the classic curve is substantially reproducible from
   regression to the mean plus better-than-average effects. The real finding is
   weaker and more interesting than the meme.
2. **Assumption-dependence.** Underdetermination and theory-ladenness: no
   observation tests a hypothesis alone. Agrippa's trilemma as the formal shape
   of the regress. Landing on fallibilism — models ranked by track record.
3. **What remains invisible.** The IIT dispute as a live case of a science
   arguing about whether its own central claim is testable at all, and the
   Cogitate adversarial collaboration as the attempt to settle it properly.
   Closes on possible-minds space: what the model predicts could exist.

- Krueger, J., & Mueller, R. A. (2002). Unskilled, unaware, or both? *Journal of
  Personality and Social Psychology*, 82(2), 180–188. **[C]**
- Kruger, J., & Dunning, D. (2002). Unskilled and unaware — but why? A reply to
  Krueger and Mueller. *JPSP*, 82(2), 189–192. **[C]**
- Burson, K. A., Larrick, R. P., & Klayman, J. (2006). Skilled or unskilled, but
  still unaware of it. *JPSP*, 90(1), 60–77. **[C]**
- Quine, W. V. O. (1951). Two dogmas of empiricism. *The Philosophical Review*,
  60(1), 20–43.
- Duhem, P. (1906/1954). *The Aim and Structure of Physical Theory*. Princeton
  University Press.
- Sextus Empiricus. *Outlines of Pyrrhonism*, I.164–169 — Agrippa's trilemma.
- Kuhn, T. S. (1962). *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions*. University of
  Chicago Press.
- Tononi, G. (2004). An information integration theory of consciousness. *BMC
  Neuroscience*, 5, 42. **[C]**
- Fleming, S., et al. (2023). The Integrated Information Theory of
  Consciousness as Pseudoscience. PsyArXiv preprint, 15 September 2023. 124
  signatories. **[C]**
- Cogitate Consortium (2025). Adversarial testing of global neuronal workspace
  and integrated information theories of consciousness. *Nature*. **[C]**

# Assessment (totals 100%)

| Task | Weight | What it tests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Ledger** — cumulative capacity/approximation/gap model, submitted three times across semester | 40% | Whether the model was built rather than recited. Graded on dependency correctness and on named gaps, not coverage. |
| **Evidence audit** — take one canonical claim about intelligence, grade it [E]/[C]/[S], defend the grade | 25% | The course's actual skill: telling a satisfying story from a supported one. |
| **A possible mind** — specify a mind the model permits, derive its blind spots, and state what would falsify your derivation | 35% | Uses the model predictively. Cannot be answered by summary. |

The final task is the course's argument in miniature: if the model is any good,
it predicts the shape of gaps in minds that do not exist yet.

# Open decisions

1. **Title.** "How and Why You're Dumb" is a strong provocation and risks
   licensing the deficiency reading the content rejects. Alternative framings
   should keep the provocation and name the compression idea.
2. **Week 1 vs Week 2 merge.** Week 1 adds no capacity. Defensible as a frame,
   but it is the weakest week structurally.
3. **Whether artificial systems appear at all.** Currently absent by design.
   They could enter week 10 (offloaded cognition) or week 12 (possible minds) —
   but only as a case *tested against* the model, never as a bolt-on.
4. **Lead lens per week** — whether to declare it on the page as a coherence
   device, which would make it testable.
